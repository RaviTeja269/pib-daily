How to read PIB without drowning

The Press Information Bureau puts out dozens of releases a day. Reading all of them is the fastest way to quit by week three. This site sorts each day's releases into read first, read next, skim and skip, and this page explains how to use that sorting.

Why PIB deserves a fixed daily slot

Drishti IAS reports that after Prelims 2026 the UPSC Chairman said most answers rested on standard textbooks, Government websites, Government press releases, and reputed newspapers. The same article argues that PIB carries the exact details a statement-based question turns on, such as target beneficiaries, funding pattern and nodal ministry, and that it covers small launches newspapers skip.

For APPSC, the Group 2 and Group 1 syllabi list current affairs of regional, national and international importance. Group 2 Mains Paper II covers the Indian and Andhra Pradesh economy and science and technology, and Paper I covers the Indian Constitution alongside the social and cultural history of Andhra Pradesh. PIB feeds the national half of that directly.

APPSC has revised its pattern and marks more than once. Check the latest notification at psc.ap.gov.in before you plan around any number.

A ten-minute routine

  1. Two minutes. Open the day's page and read only the titles under Read first.
  2. Five minutes. Open each linked release. Find who, what, how much and why now. For a scheme, copy the salient features as they are written; they read like ready-made exam statements.
  3. Two minutes. Run down the Read next titles. Open only what you cannot place in the syllabus.
  4. One minute. Choose Save on two or three items and write one line you will recognise in the exam.

On a weekend, switch to the 7 days view, open Saved, and read your own one-liners instead of new material. With thirty minutes, add the Skim list.

The yellow chips under each title are names and acronyms pulled from the text. They are a starting list, not a complete one.

What to skip

Skip courtesy calls, greetings, birthday messages, routine inspections, ceremonial events and administrative notices such as exam schedules and campaign status reports. Drishti adds a caution worth keeping: ignore a standard political speech unless it announces a major policy shift. The skip list on each day is collapsed but never hidden, so open it once a week and check the filter did not bury something.

Ministries to read first

Drishti suggests starting with the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs, Finance, Environment, Forest and Climate Change, and Science and Technology, and lists External Affairs, Agriculture and Education among the ministries to prioritise. This site gives each ministry a starting weight so the same ones surface first.

Weekly and yearly extras

  • The PIB Research Unit publishes explainers worth a weekend slot: Backgrounders, Factsheets and Features.
  • Ministries publish Year-End Reviews in December and January. Drishti calls them arguably the highest-yield documents for Mains revision. This site gives them extra weight.

The APPSC lens

  • Choose APPSC under "Prepare for" to push Andhra Pradesh items, Economy, Science and Tech and Polity up the list.
  • An item gets the Andhra Pradesh tag when its text mentions a place or product from a list in config.json, such as Amaravati, Polavaram, Sriharikota, Kondapalli or Etikoppaka, or when it comes from PIB Vijayawada. Add your own words to that list.
  • PIB's Vijayawada office has its own RSS feed. Add it under extra_feeds in config.json to catch regional releases the national feed misses (the README shows how to find the address).
  • If the AI notes are switched on, each important release also gets a short Telugu summary.

How items are scored

Every release gets a score. It starts with the ministry weight above, then adds points for signals in the title and text and subtracts points for routine events. A signal in the title counts in full; one found only in the body counts half.

SignalPoints
Cabinet decision+4
Year-end review+3
Exercise+3
Scheme or portal launch, recognition or tag, space mission+2.5
MoU, notification, law, report or index, guidelines, bilateral outcome, explainer+2
Andhra Pradesh link+1.5
Courtesy call, greetings, admin notice−3
Routine campaign−2.5
Reviews, visits, inspections, meetings−1

A score of 7 or more is read first, 5 or more is read next, 3 or more is skim, and anything lower is skip. The numbers are judgment calls, not official categories. They live in config.json, so when the site misses something you wanted, change the weight or add a keyword and the next run uses it.
